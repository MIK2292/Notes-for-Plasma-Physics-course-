# From the ensemble to Vlasov equation
We begin our discussion considering an ensemble of particles 
PIC ensemble

with a fixed number of $N$ particles which have position $x_{i}$ and velocities $v_{i}$. 
We could study them in a Lagrangian form
$$
 \begin{cases}
\dot{\bar{x}} _{i} = \bar{v}_{i} \\[.6em]
\dot{\bar{v}} _{i } = \bar{a}_{i}
\end{cases}
$$
with unspecified acceleration $\bar{a}_{i}$.
We could aso use the Eulerian study in one spatial dimension and one velocity dimension (1d 1v system).
We will have a phase space at every time $t$.
PIC phase space

We need to reduce the degree of freedom by disctretizing the 1d 1v dimensions (course grain of phase space)
PIC grid on phase space 

We can choose an arbitrary $\Delta x$ and $\Delta v$ 
In one cell $\Delta x \Delta v$ we have $\Delta N < \infty$ number of particles
$$
 \Delta N = \sum f(\bar{x},\bar{v},t) \,\Delta x \Delta v
$$
with $f(\bar{x},\bar{v},t)$ being the probability (or velocity) distribution function.
The master equation is 
$$
 \frac{d}{dt} f(\bar{x}(t),\bar{v}(t),t) = 0
$$
$$
 \frac{ \partial f }{ \partial t } + \frac{ \partial \bar{x} }{ \partial t } \frac{ \partial f }{ \partial \bar{x} } +  \frac{ \partial \bar{v} }{ \partial t } \frac{ \partial f }{ \partial \bar{v} } = 0
$$
Which gets us to the Vlasov equation
$$
 \frac{ \partial f }{ \partial t }  + \bar{v} \cdot \nabla f + \bar{a} \cdot \nabla_{\bar{v}} f =0
$$

# The different moments of Vlasov equation
### Zeroth moment
If we consider different species (with different index $\alpha$), we will have 
$$
 \begin{align}
 f_{\alpha} (\bar{x},\bar{v},t); && n_{\alpha} (\bar{x},t) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f_{\alpha} (\bar{x},\bar{v},t) \, d^3v
\end{align}
$$
with $n_{\alpha}(\bar{x},t)$ being the density of particles (zeroth moment).
We also have the total number of particles in the system at a given time (which is adimensional)
$$
 N_{\alpha} (t) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} n_{\alpha(\bar{x},t)} \, d^3x 
$$
We can do a dimensional analisys on $f$ 
$$
 [f] = \frac{\#}{L^3 \left( L / T \right)^3 } = \# \frac{T^3}{L^6}
$$
### First moments
Velocity 
$$
 \bar{v} (\bar{x},t) = \frac{ \int \bar{v} f(\bar{x},\bar{v},t) \, d^3v }{\int f(\bar{x},\bar{v},t) \, d^3v} = \frac{ \int \bar{v} f(\bar{x},\bar{v},t) \, d^3v }{n (\bar{x},t)}
$$
We note that the variable $n$ at the denominator means that we are not allowed to work in vacuum (with $n(\bar{x},t) = 0$). In that case we need some new physics. 
PIC bulk velocity

We define the bulk velocity $\bar{U}$ as the mean velocities of the particles over a small region.
$\bar{U} =0$ does not mean that particles are not moving. 
In a closed bottle the air can reach a fraction of the light speed, but the bulk velocity will still be zero anyways.

# The two fluids equations
We take the Vlasov-Mawell equations (for $\alpha = p,e \to f_{\alpha} (\bar{x},\bar{v},t)$) 
$$
\begin{align}
 &\frac{ \partial f _{\alpha}}{ \partial t }  + \bar{v} \cdot \nabla f_{\alpha } + \frac{q_{\alpha}}{m_{\alpha}} \left[ \hat{E} + \bar{V} \times \bar{B} \right] \cdot \nabla _{\bar{v}} f_{\alpha} = 0  \\[.8em]
&\begin{cases}
\nabla \cdot \bar{E} = \dfrac{1}{\varepsilon_{0}} \sum_{\alpha} q_{\alpha} n_{\alpha} = \dfrac{1}{\varepsilon_{0}} \sum_{\alpha} \rho_{c,\alpha}  \\[.8em]
\nabla \times \bar{E} = - \dfrac{ \partial \bar{B} }{ \partial t } \\[.8em]
 \nabla \times \bar{B} = \mu_{0} \bar{J} + \mu_{0} \varepsilon_{0} \dfrac{ \partial \bar{E} }{ \partial t } 
\end{cases}
\end{align}
$$
(the right hand side of the Vlasov equation equal to zero means that we have no collisions)
We define 
$$
 \begin{align}
\rho_{m, \alpha} = m_{\alpha} n_{\alpha} ;&&  
\rho_{c, \alpha} = q_{\alpha} n_{\alpha} ;&&
\bar{J} = \sum_{\alpha} q_{\alpha} m_{\alpha} \bar{U}_{\alpha}
\end{align}
$$
Now we integrate in the velocities and we move to the two fluids description.
### Zeroth moment equation
$$
 \int \left\{ \frac{ \partial f _{\alpha}}{ \partial t }  + \bar{v} \cdot \nabla f_{\alpha } + \frac{q_{\alpha}}{m_{\alpha}} \left[ \bar{E} + \bar{V} \times \bar{B} \right] \cdot \nabla _{\bar{v}} f_{\alpha} \right\} \, d^3 v = 0
$$
Wich we solve one integral at a time
The first one is
$$
\int  \frac{ \partial f _{\alpha}}{ \partial t } \, d^3 v = \frac{ \partial  }{ \partial t } \int f_{\alpha} \, d^3v = \frac{ \partial n_{\alpha} (\bar{x},t) }{ \partial t } 
$$
The second integral is 
$$
 \int  \bar{v} \cdot \nabla f_{\alpha }  \, d^3 v = \nabla \cdot \left\{ \int \bar{v} f_{\alpha} \, d^3v  \right\}  = \nabla \cdot (n_{\alpha} \bar{U}_{\alpha})
$$
To solve the third integral we note that $\bar{E}$ and $\bar{B}$ are independent of the velocity. 
So $\bar{V} \times \bar{B}$ is perpendicular to the velocity. 
The third integral reduces to
$$
\begin{align}
 \int  \frac{q_{\alpha}}{m_{\alpha}} &\left[ \bar{E} + \bar{V} \times \bar{B} \right] \cdot \nabla _{\bar{v}} f_{\alpha}  \, d^3 v = \\[.8em]
&= \frac{q_{\alpha}}{m_{\alpha}} \int \nabla_{\bar{v}} \cdot \left\{ \left[ \bar{E} + \bar{V} \times \bar{B} \right]  f_{\alpha} \right\} \, d^3v =  \\[.8em]
&= \frac{q_{\alpha}}{m_{\alpha}} \left. \left[ \bar{E} + \bar{V} \times \bar{B} \right] f_{\alpha} \right|_{v \to \pm \infty} =  \\[.8em]
&= 0
\end{align}
$$
Because $f_{\alpha}$ goes to zero at infinity faster than the other terms. 
So we get 
$$
 \frac{ \partial n_{\alpha} }{ \partial t } + \nabla \cdot (n_{\alpha} \bar{U}_{\alpha}) =0
$$
We are facing now a **closure problem**, we have a moment which depends on the next moment. 
### First moment equation
The first moment equation will be given by
$$
 \int \left\{ \bar{v}\cdot \frac{ \partial f _{\alpha}}{ \partial t }  + \bar{v} \cdot \nabla (f_{\alpha }\bar{v}) + \frac{q_{\alpha}}{m_{\alpha}} \bar{v} \cdot\nabla _{\bar{v}} \left[  \left( \bar{E} + \bar{V} \times \bar{B} \right) f_{\alpha}  \right]\right\} \, d^3 v = 0 
$$
The first integral is 
$$
 \int \bar{v}\cdot \frac{ \partial f _{\alpha}}{ \partial t }  \, d^3 v =  \int \frac{ \partial  }{ \partial t } (f_{\alpha}\bar{v}) \, d^3v = \frac{ \partial }{ \partial t } \left( n_{\alpha} \bar{U}_{\alpha} \right) 
$$
The second integral is 
$$
  \int  \bar{v} \cdot \nabla (f_{\alpha }\bar{v}) \, d^3 v = \int  \nabla \cdot (f_{\alpha }\bar{v}\bar{v}) \, d^3 v
$$
with 
$$
f_{\alpha }\bar{v}\bar{v} = 
\begin{pmatrix}
f_{\alpha} v_{x} v_{x}  & f_{\alpha} v_{x} v_{y} \\
f_{\alpha} v_{y} v_{x} & f_{\alpha} v_{y} v_{y}
\end{pmatrix}
$$
We now define the **pressure tensor** 
$$
\begin{align}
 \stackrel{\leftrightarrow}{P^\alpha} &= m_{\alpha} \int \left( \bar{v} - \bar{U}_{\alpha} \right) \left( \bar{v} - \bar{U}_{\alpha} \right) f_{\alpha} \, d^3v = \\[.8em]
&=m_{\alpha} \int \left[ \bar{v} \bar{v} - \bar{U}_{\alpha} \bar{v} - \bar{v} \bar{U}_{\alpha} + \bar{U}_{\alpha}\bar{U}_{\alpha} \right] f_{\alpha} \, d^3v = \\[.8em] 
&=m_{\alpha} \int \bar{v} \bar{v} f_{\alpha} \, d^3v - m_{\alpha} n_{\alpha} \bar{U}_{\alpha} \bar{U}_{\alpha} \pm \cancel{ m_{\alpha} n_{\alpha} \bar{U}_{\alpha} \bar{U}_{\alpha} }= \\[.8em]
&= m_{\alpha} \int \bar{v} \bar{v} f_{\alpha} \, d^3v - m_{\alpha} n_{\alpha} \bar{U}_{\alpha} \bar{U}_{\alpha}
\end{align}
$$
So finally, the second integral is 
$$
  \int  \bar{v} \cdot \nabla (f_{\alpha }\bar{v}) \, d^3 v = \int  \nabla \cdot(f_{\alpha }\bar{v}\bar{v}) \, d^3 v = \nabla \cdot \left[ \frac{\stackrel{\leftrightarrow}{P^\alpha}}{m_{\alpha}} + n_{\alpha} \bar{U}_{\alpha} \bar{U}_{\alpha} \right] 
$$
For the third integral we have (solving by parts)
$$
\begin{align}
 \int  \frac{q_{\alpha}}{m_{\alpha}} \bar{v} &\cdot\nabla _{\bar{v}}  \left[  \left( \bar{E} + \bar{V} \times \bar{B} \right) f_{\alpha}  \right] \, d^3 v =  \\[.8em]
&= \frac{q_{\alpha}}{m_{\alpha}}  \left\{ \left. \bar{v} \left[  \left( \bar{E} + \bar{V} \times \bar{B} \right) f_{\alpha} \right] \cdot\hat{n} \right|_{-\infty}^{+\infty} - \int \left( \bar{E} + \bar{V} \times \bar{B} \right) f_{\alpha} \, d^3v    \right\} =  \\[.8em]
 &= \frac{q_{\alpha}}{m_{\alpha}} n_{\alpha} \left[ \bar{E} + \bar{U}_{\alpha} \times \bar{B} \right] 
\end{align}
$$
### The final two fluid equations
So in the end we have
$$
\frac{ \partial }{ \partial t } \left( n_{\alpha} \bar{U}_{\alpha} \right) + \nabla \cdot \left[ \frac{\stackrel{\leftrightarrow}{P^\alpha}}{m_{\alpha}} + n_{\alpha} \bar{U}_{\alpha} \bar{U}_{\alpha} \right] + \frac{q_{\alpha}}{m_{\alpha}} n_{\alpha} \left[ \bar{E} + \bar{U}_{\alpha} \times \bar{B} \right]  =0
$$
with the pressure tensor being
$$
 \stackrel{\leftrightarrow}{P^\alpha} = P^{\alpha}_{ij} = \begin{pmatrix}
P_{xx}^\alpha  & P_{xy}^\alpha  & \dots\\
P_{yx}^\alpha  & P_{yy}^\alpha & \dots \\
\dots & \dots & \dots
\end{pmatrix}
$$
Wich is symmetric and diagonalizable.
"Pressure $P$ and temperature $T$ are the same"
It can be:
**Isotropic** 
It has equal entries on the diagonal ($\stackrel{\leftrightarrow}{P^\alpha} = T\mathbb{1}$)
Spherical symmetry
PIC isotropic


**Anisotropic**
$T_{xx} > T_{yy}$
PIC anisotroic

**Agyrotropic**
$T_{xx} \neq T_{yy} \neq T_{zz}$ 
It has the shape of a confetto, ellissoide with three different axis.
Real data from space gives all terms of the pressure tensor different from zero.


We will study the easiest case with $\stackrel{\leftrightarrow}{P^\alpha} = P_{\alpha}\mathbb{1}$. 
The final system will be 
$$
 \begin{cases}
 \dfrac{ \partial n_{\alpha} }{ \partial t } + \nabla \cdot (n_{\alpha} \bar{U}_{\alpha}) =0 \\[.8em]
\displaystyle\frac{ \partial }{ \partial t } \left( m_{\alpha} n_{\alpha} \bar{U}_{\alpha} \right) + \nabla \cdot \left[m_{\alpha} n_{\alpha} \bar{U}_{\alpha} \bar{U}_{\alpha} \right] + \nabla P_{\alpha}+ q_{\alpha} n_{\alpha} \left[ \bar{E} + \bar{U}_{\alpha} \times \bar{B} \right]  =0
\end{cases}
$$
which we close with an Equation of State
$$
 P_{\alpha} = An_{\alpha}^\gamma
$$
plus the Maxwell equations.
