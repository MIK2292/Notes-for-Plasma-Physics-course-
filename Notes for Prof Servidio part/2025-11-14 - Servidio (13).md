# Quadratic invariance of incompressible ideal MHD
Quadratic form of MHD invariance
(Biskmap book)

**First invariant** 
Energy
$$
 E \equiv \left\langle  \frac{v^2+ b^2}{2}  \right\rangle = \frac{1}{V} \int\,d^3x \left[ \frac{U^2(\bar{x},t)}{2} + \frac{B^2(\bar{x},t)}{2} \right] 
$$
which has an apparent time dependency. 

**Second invariant** 
Cross helicity
$$
 H_{c} \equiv \left\langle   \frac{\bar{U} \cdot \bar{B}}{2}  \right\rangle 
$$

**Third invariant** 
Magnetic helicity
$$
 H_{m} \equiv \left\langle  \frac{\bar{a}\cdot \bar{b}}{2}  \right\rangle 
$$
with $\bar{a}$ as the magnetic potential
$$
 \nabla \times \bar{a} = \bar{b}
$$
Usually $\bar{b}$ is the fluctuation of magnetic field, while $\bar{B}$ is the full magnetic field. 
We use the following hypotesys
$$
 \langle \bar{U} \rangle = \langle \bar{b} \rangle = 0
$$
MHD is galileian invariant. 

## Conservation of energy
$$
\frac{d}{dt} E(t) = 0 
$$
$$
\begin{align}
 \frac{d}{dt} E &=  \frac{d}{dt}  \left\langle   \frac{U^2(\bar{x},t)}{2}  + \frac{b^2(\bar{x},t)}{2}  \right\rangle = \\[.8em]
&= \frac{d}{dt}  \left\{ \frac{1}{V} \int \, d^3x \left[ \frac{U^2(\bar{x},t)}{2}  + \frac{b^2(\bar{x},t)}{2} \right] \right\}  =  \\[.8em]
&= \frac{1}{V} \int \, d^3x \frac{ \partial  }{ \partial t } \left[  \frac{U^2(\bar{x},t)}{2}  + \frac{b^2(\bar{x},t)}{2} \right]  \\[.8em]
&= \left\langle  \frac{1}{2} \frac{ \partial  }{ \partial t } \left[  U^2(\bar{x},t)  + b^2(\bar{x},t) \right]  \right\rangle =  \\[.8em]
&= \left\langle  \bar{U} \cdot \frac{ \partial \bar{U} }{ \partial t } + \bar{b} \cdot \frac{ \partial \bar{b} }{ \partial t }   \right\rangle 
\end{align}
$$
We use incompressible MHD equations.
HERE why we sub $\bar{B}$ with $\bar{b}$ without any correction? or why $(\nabla \times  \bar{b}) \times \bar{b} = (\nabla \times  \bar{B}) \times \bar{B}$?
$$
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{ \partial \bar{U} }{ \partial t } = -\bar{U} \cdot \nabla \bar{U} - \nabla P + (\nabla \times  \bar{b}) \times \bar{b} \\[.8em]
\dfrac{ \partial \bar{b} }{ \partial t } = \nabla \times (\bar{U} \times \bar{b}) = - \bar{U} \cdot \nabla \bar{B} + \bar{B} \cdot \nabla \bar{U}
\end{cases}
$$
We substitute
$$
\begin{align}
\bar{U} \cdot \frac{ \partial \bar{U} }{ \partial t }  &= \bar{U} \cdot [- \bar{U} \cdot \nabla \bar{U} + (\nabla \times \bar{b})\times \bar{b} - \nabla P ] =  \\[.8em] 
&=  - \bar{U} \cdot [\bar{U} \cdot \nabla \bar{U}] + \bar{U} \cdot [(\nabla \times \bar{b})\times \bar{b}] - \bar{U} \cdot\nabla P 
\end{align}
$$
We have incompressibility, so 
$$
\begin{align}
 \nabla \cdot \bar{U} = 0&& \implies &&\bar{U} \cdot \nabla \bar{U} = \nabla \cdot (\bar{U} \bar{U})
\end{align}
$$
(we can move $\bar{U}$ in divergence without any correction term)
and
$$
\bar{U} \cdot [\bar{U} \cdot \nabla \bar{U}] = \bar{U} \cdot [\nabla \cdot (\bar{U} \bar{U})] = \nabla \cdot (U^2\bar{U})
$$
and also 
$$
\begin{align}
 \bar{U} \cdot [(\nabla \times \bar{b}) \times \bar{b}] &= \bar{U} \cdot \left[  \bar{b} \cdot \nabla\bar{b}  - \nabla \left( \frac{b^2}{2} \right) \right] = \\[.8em]
& = \bar{U} \cdot \left[ \nabla \cdot (\bar{b} \bar{b}) - \nabla \left( \frac{b^2}{2} \right)  \right] =  \\[.8em]
&= \nabla \cdot [(\bar{U} \cdot \bar{b}) \bar{b}] - \nabla \cdot \left( \frac{b^2}{2} \bar{U} \right) =  \\[.8em]
&= \nabla \cdot \left[ (\bar{U} \cdot \bar{b} ) \bar{b} - \frac{b^2}{2}\bar{U} \right] 

\end{align}
$$
and also 
$$
\bar{U} \cdot \nabla P = \nabla \cdot (P\bar{U}) 
$$
So we have 
$$
\bar{U} \cdot \dfrac{ \partial \bar{U} }{ \partial t } = \nabla \cdot \left\{ -U^2 \bar{U} + (\bar{U} \cdot \bar{b})\bar{b} - \frac{b^2}{2} \bar{U}  - P\bar{U}\right\}
$$
We also have (using $\nabla \cdot \bar{U} = \nabla \cdot \bar{b} = 0$) 
$$
\begin{align}
\bar{b} \cdot \dfrac{ \partial \bar{b} }{ \partial t } &= \bar{b} \cdot \left[  \nabla \times (\bar{U} \times \bar{b}) \right] = \\[.8em]
&= \bar{b} \cdot \left[ - \bar{U} \cdot \nabla \bar{b} + \bar{b} \cdot \nabla \bar{U}  \right] =  \\[.8em]
&= \bar{b} \cdot \left[  - \nabla \cdot (\bar{U} \bar{b}) + \nabla \cdot (\bar{b} \bar{U}) \right] =  \\[.8em]
 &= \nabla \cdot \left[  - (\bar{b} \cdot \bar{U}) \bar{b} + b^2 \bar{U} \right] = \\[.8em]
 &= -\nabla \cdot \left[ (\bar{b} \cdot \bar{U} )\bar{b} - b^2 \bar{U} \right] 
\end{align}
$$
So 
$$
\begin{align}
 \bar{U} \cdot \frac{ \partial \bar{U} }{ \partial t } + \bar{b} \cdot \frac{ \partial \bar{b} }{ \partial t } &= \nabla \cdot  \left\{ - U^2 \bar{U} + \cancel{ (\bar{U} \cdot \bar{b}) \bar{b} } - \frac{b^2}{2} \bar{U} - P\bar{U}- \cancel{ (\bar{b} \cdot \bar{U} )\bar{b} } + b^2 \bar{U}  \right\} =  \\[.8em]
&= \nabla \cdot \left\{ \bar{U} \left( \frac{b^2}{2} - U^2 \right)  - P\bar{U}\right\} 
\end{align}
$$
we can use the divergence theorem
$$
\begin{align}
 \frac{d}{dt}  E &= \frac{1}{V} \int \nabla \cdot \left\{ \left( \frac{b^2}{2} - U^2 \right) \bar{U} - P\bar{U}  \right\} \, d^3x =  \int \bar{M} \cdot \hat{n} \, d\Sigma
\end{align}
$$
with 
$$
 \bar{M} \equiv \left( \frac{b^2}{2} - U^2 \right) \bar{U} - P\bar{U}
$$
At boundaries $U_{n} \equiv \bar{U} \cdot \hat{n}  = 0$ 
And we have 
$$
\begin{align}
\frac{d}{dt}  E(t) = 0  && \Longleftrightarrow && U_{n} = 0
\end{align}
$$

## Conservation of cross helicity
Cross helicity tells us how much $\bar{U}$ and $\bar{b}$ are aligned, averaged in space.
Recall
$$
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{ \partial \bar{v} }{ \partial t } = -\bar{U} \cdot \nabla \bar{U} - \nabla P + (\nabla \times  \bar{b}) \times \bar{b} && (\text{Euler equation})\\[.8em]
\dfrac{ \partial \bar{b} }{ \partial t } = \nabla \times (\bar{U} \times \bar{b}) = - \bar{U} \cdot \nabla \bar{B} + \bar{B} \cdot \nabla \bar{U} && (\text{Ampère-Maxwell law})
\end{cases}
$$
($\mathbb{1}$ is the identity tensor)
$$
\begin{align}
\frac{d}{dt}  H_{e} &= \frac{d}{dt}  \left< \frac{\bar{U} \cdot \bar{b}}{2} \right> = \frac{1}{2} \left<  \bar{b} \cdot \frac{ \partial \bar{U} }{ \partial t } + \bar{U} \cdot \frac{ \partial \bar{b} }{ \partial t }  \right> = \\[.8em]
&= \frac{1}{2} \left<  \bar{b} \cdot \nabla \cdot \left[ - \bar{U} \bar{U} - P \mathbb{1} + \bar{b} \bar{b} - \frac{b^2}{2} \mathbb{1} \right] + \bar{U}\cdot \nabla \cdot \left[ - \bar{U} \bar{b} + \bar{b} \bar{U} \right]  \right> =  \\[.8em]
&= \frac{1}{2} \left<  \nabla \cdot \left[ - \cancel{ (\bar{U} \cdot \bar{b} ) \bar{U} } - P \bar{b} + b^2 \bar{b} - \frac{b^2}{2}\bar{b} - U^2 \bar{b}+  \cancel{ \bar{U} \cdot \bar{b} \bar{U} } \right]  \right>  =\\[.8em]
&= -\frac{1}{2} \left<  \nabla \cdot \left[ \bar{b} \left( P-\frac{b^2}{2} -U^2 \right) \right]   \right> =\\[.8em]
&= -\frac{1}{2} \left<  \nabla \cdot \bar{A}   \right> 
\end{align}
$$
with 
$$
 \bar{A} \equiv \bar{b} \left( P-\frac{b^2}{2} -U^2 \right)
$$
so we have
$$
\begin{align}
 \frac{d}{dt}  H_{e} = -\frac{1}{2V} \int d^3x
 \, \nabla \cdot \bar{A} = 0 && \Longleftrightarrow && b_{n } = 0
\end{align}
$$

## Conservation of magnetic helicity
$$
\bar{a}: \ \ \ \ \nabla \times \bar{a} = \bar{b}
$$
We have a gauge on $\bar{a}$ 
$$
\bar{a} = \bar{a}' + \nabla \phi
$$
with 
$$
\begin{align}
\bar{b} = \nabla \times \bar{a} && \implies && \nabla \times \nabla \phi = 0
\end{align}
$$
and
$$
\begin{align}
\bar{b} = \nabla \times \bar{a} = \nabla \times \bar{a}' \to \nabla \cdot \bar{a} = 0 && \Longleftrightarrow && \nabla \cdot \bar{a}' = - \nabla ^2 \phi
\end{align}
$$
So we evaluate
$$
\frac{d}{dt}  H_{m} (t) = \frac{d}{dt}  \left<  \frac{\bar{a}\cdot \bar{b}}{2} \right> =  \frac{1}{2} \left<  \frac{ \partial (\bar{a} \cdot \bar{b}) }{ \partial t }  \right> = \frac{1}{2} \left< \bar{a} \frac{ \partial \bar{b} }{ \partial t } + \bar{b} \cdot \frac{ \partial \bar{a} }{ \partial t }  \right>
$$
We can write (using Ampère-Maxwell law)
$$
 \frac{ \partial \bar{b} }{ \partial t } = - \nabla \times \bar{E} = \nabla \times (\bar{U} \times \bar{b}) 
$$
with
$$
\bar{E} = - \bar{U} \times \bar{b}
$$
and we also get 
$$
 \frac{ \partial \bar{b} }{ \partial t } =\frac{ \partial (\nabla \times \bar{a}) }{ \partial t } =  \nabla \times \left( \frac{ \partial \bar{a} }{ \partial t }  \right) 
$$
so we obtain 
$$
\frac{ \partial \bar{a} }{ \partial t } = \bar{U} \times \bar{b}+ \nabla \psi 
$$
($\psi$ is any function differentiable and continuous in the considered volume).
So
$$
 \begin{align}
\frac{ \partial (\bar{a} \cdot \bar{b}) }{ \partial t } &= \bar{a} \cdot \frac{ \partial \bar{b} }{ \partial t }  +  \bar{b} \cdot \frac{ \partial \bar{a} }{ \partial t } =  \\[.8em]
&= -\bar{a} \cdot \left[ -\bar{U} \cdot \nabla \bar{b} + \bar{b} \cdot \nabla \bar{U} \right] + \bar{b} \cdot \left[(\bar{U} \times \bar{b}) + \nabla \phi \right] =  \\[.8em]
&= \nabla \cdot \left[ (\bar{a} \cdot \bar{b}) \bar{U} - (\bar{a}\cdot \bar{U} )\bar{b} + \phi \bar{b} \right] 
\end{align}
$$
where the third term is null.

And finally 
$$
 \frac{d}{dt}  H_{m} = \frac{d}{dt}  \left<  \frac{\bar{a} \cdot \bar{b}}{2} \right> = \frac{1}{2V} \int d^3x \, \nabla \cdot \left[ (\bar{a}\cdot \bar{b}) \bar{U} - (\bar{a}\cdot \bar{U}) \bar{b} + \phi \bar{b} \right] = \frac{1}{2V} \int d^3x \, \nabla \cdot \bar{S}
$$
$$
\begin{align}
\frac{d}{dt}  H_{m} = 0 && \Longleftrightarrow && S_{n} = 0  && \Longleftrightarrow &&\begin{cases}
U_{n} = 0 \\[.6em]
B_{n} = 0
\end{cases}
\end{align}
$$
(also they balance out, but it's a very particular case) 
Magnetic helicity measures twistedness, how much the magnetic field is twisting.

# Momentum equation for non-ideal, incompresible MHD
We forget for a moment the magnetic field
PIC sheet of fluid
![[IMG_20251114_113937462_HDR~2.jpg|400]]
We have an infinitesiml surface of fluid, we decompose the internal forces acting on $\Delta S$ (the infinitesimal surface) in $F_{\parallel}$ to the surface and $F_{\perp}$ to the surface. 
We have the following definitions for the pressure ($P$) and tagential stress ($\sigma_{t}$).
$$
\begin{align}
P = \lim_{ \delta S  \to 0 } \frac{\Delta F_{n}}{\Delta S};&&\sigma_{t} = \lim_{ \Delta S \to 0 }  \frac{\Delta F_{t}}{\Delta S}
\end{align}
$$
If $\nu = 0$ then $\sigma_{t} = 0$. 

The stress tensor $\stackrel{\leftrightarrow}\tau = \tau_{ij}$ is
$$
\tau_{ij} = -P \delta_{ij} + \frac{\nu}{2} \left( \frac{ \partial U_{i} }{ \partial x_{j} } +\frac{ \partial U_{j} }{ \partial x_{i} }  \right)
$$
and (using $\nabla \cdot \stackrel{\leftrightarrow}\tau = \tau_{ij,i} \, \hat{e}_{j}$)
$$
\begin{align}
\frac{ \partial \bar{U} }{ \partial t } &= - \bar{U} \cdot \nabla \bar{U} + \nabla \cdot \stackrel{\leftrightarrow}\tau + (\nabla \times \bar{b}) \times \bar{b} =  \\[.8em]
&=  - \bar{U} \cdot \nabla \bar{U} - \nabla P + \nu \nabla ^2 \bar{U} + (\nabla \times \bar{b})\times \bar{b}

\end{align}
$$
where the term with the laplacian is the non-linear term.

Recall that
$$
 \begin{align}
0 = \nabla \cdot \bar{U} = \frac{ \partial U_{i} }{ \partial x_{i} }; && 0= \nabla \left( \nabla \cdot \bar{U} \right) = \frac{ \partial }{ \partial x_{j} } \frac{ \partial U_{i} }{ \partial x_{i} } \hat{e}_{j}= \frac{ \partial^2 U_{i} }{ \partial x_{j} \partial x_{i} } \hat{e}_{j}
\end{align}
$$
so
$$
\begin{align}
\nabla \cdot \stackrel{\leftrightarrow}\tau &= \tau_{ij,i} \, \hat{e}_{j} = 
\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}} \left\{-P \delta_{ij} + \frac{\nu}{2} \left( \frac{ \partial U_{i} }{ \partial x_{j} } +\frac{ \partial U_{j} }{ \partial x_{i} }  \right) \right\}  \hat{e}_{j}=  \\[.8em]
&= -\frac{ \partial  }{ \partial x_{i} }P \hat{e}_{i} + \cancel{ \frac{\nu}{2} \frac{ \partial^2 U_{i} }{ \partial x_{i} \partial x_{j} } \hat{e}_{j} } + \frac{\nu}{2} \frac{ \partial^2 U_{j} }{ \partial x_{i}^2 } \hat{e}_{j} = \\[.8em]
&= - \nabla P + \nu \nabla^2 \bar{U}
\end{align}
$$
where in the last step we used
$$
\begin{align}
\nabla P = \frac{ \partial  }{ \partial x_{i} }P \hat{e}_{i}; &&  \nabla ^2 \bar{U} = \frac{ \partial^2  }{ \partial x_{i}^2 } U_{j} \hat{e}_{j}
\end{align}
$$

Induction equation for non-ideal MHD
We use  Ohm's law
PIC ohm conductor tube

$$
\begin{align}
 E\Delta x = \Delta V = Ri = R \bar{J} \cdot \bar{V} &&  \implies&& \bar{E} = \eta \bar{J}
\end{align}
$$
with $\eta$ resistivity
So the new, non ideal Ohm's law is
$$
\bar{E} = - \bar{U} \times \bar{b} + \eta \bar{J}
$$
from which 
$$
\begin{align}
\frac{ \partial \bar{b} }{ \partial t } &= - \nabla \times \bar{E} = \nabla \times \left[ \bar{U} \times \bar{b} - \eta \bar{J} \right] = \\[.8em]
& = \nabla \times (\bar{U} \times \bar{b} ) - \eta \nabla \times (\nabla \times \bar{b}) =   \\[.8em]
 &= \nabla \times (\bar{U} \times \bar{b} ) - \eta \nabla ^2 \bar{b} + \eta \nabla \cancel{ (\nabla \cdot \bar{b}) } =  \\[.8em]
&= \nabla \times (\bar{U} \times \bar{b} ) - \eta \nabla ^2 \bar{b}
\end{align}$$
So our non ideal, visco-resistive, incompressible MHD 
$$
 \begin{cases}
\dfrac{ \partial \bar{U} }{ \partial t } = - \bar{U} \cdot \nabla \bar{U} - \nabla P + (\nabla \times \bar{b}) \times \bar{b} + \nu \nabla ^2 \bar{U}  \\[.8em]
\dfrac{ \partial \bar{b} }{ \partial t } = \nabla \times (\bar{U} \times \bar{b}) + \eta \nabla ^2 \bar{b}  
\end{cases}
$$
