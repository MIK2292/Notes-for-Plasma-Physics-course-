## Klimontovich derivation
System of $N_{0}$ particles with position $\bar{x}_i (t)$ and velocity $\bar{v}_{i}(t)$ 
Equation of motion
$$
\begin{cases}
\dot{\bar{x}} _{i} (t)= \bar{v}_{i} (t) \\[.8em]
\displaystyle \dot{\bar{v}} _{i} (t)= \frac{\rho_{s}}{m_{s}} \left[ \bar{E}_{m} (\bar{x}_{i}(t),t) + \frac{\bar{v}\times \bar{B}_{m}(\bar{x}_{i}(t),t)}{c}\right] 
\end{cases}
$$
and Maxwell equations
$$
 \begin{cases}
\displaystyle \nabla \cdot \bar{E}_{m} (\bar{x}_{i}(t),t) = 4 \pi \rho_{m}  (\bar{x}_{i}(t),t)\\[1em]
\nabla \cdot \bar{B}_{m} (\bar{x}_{i}(t),t) = 0 \\[0.6em]
\nabla \times \bar{E}_{m} (\bar{x}_{i}(t),t) = - \dfrac{1}{c} \dfrac{ \partial \bar{B}_{m} (\bar{x}_{i}(t),t) }{ \partial t } \\[0.6em]
 \displaystyle\nabla \times \bar{B}_{m}(\bar{x}_{i}(t),t) = \dfrac{4\pi}{c} \bar{J}_{m}(\bar{x}_{i}(t),t) +  \dfrac{1}{c} \dfrac{ \partial \bar{E}_{m} (\bar{x}_{i}(t),t)}{ \partial t } 
 \end{cases}
$$
Phase space density $N_{s}$ 
$$
N_{s} \left( \bar{x} ,\bar{v},t\right) = \sum_{i=1}^{N_{0}} \delta \left( \bar{x} - \bar{x}_{i}(t) \right) \, \delta\left( \bar{v} - \bar{v}_{i}(t) \right)  
$$
Charge density
$$
\rho_{m}(\bar{x},t) = \sum_{s} q_{s} \int N_{s}  \left( \bar{x} ,\bar{v},t\right)\, d\bar{v} 
$$
Current
$$
\bar{J} _{m}   (\bar{x},t) = \sum_{s} q_{s} \int \bar{v} N_{s} (\bar{x},\bar{v},t) \, d\bar{v}
$$
$N_{s}$ is a "very spiky" function, which enters the Maxwell equations

Let's have a look at some $\delta(x)$ properties:
1) $\displaystyle \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}  \delta(x) \, dx = 1$ 
2) $\displaystyle \int \delta (x-a) \, dx = 1, \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \  \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \  \ \ \ \forall a \in \mathbb{R}$   
3) $\displaystyle \int \delta (x-a) \, f(x) \, dx = f(a), \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \forall a \in n \mathbb{R}$   
4) $\displaystyle f(a) \,\delta (a-b)  = f(b)\, \delta (a-b), \ \ \ \ \ \forall a,b \in \mathbb{R}$ 

Now (using property n.$2$)
$$
\begin{align}
N_{0} &= \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} N_{s}(\bar{x},\bar{v},t) \, d\bar{x} \, d\bar{v} = \\[.8em]
 &= \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \sum_{i=1}^{N_{0}} \delta \left( \bar{x} - \bar{x}_{i} \right) \, \delta\left( \bar{v} - \bar{v}_{i} \right)   \, d\bar{x} \, d\bar{v} = \\[.8em]
&=  \sum_{i=1}^{N_{0}} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \delta \left( \bar{x} - \bar{x}_{i} \right) \, d\bar{x} \, \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\delta\left( \bar{v} - \bar{v}_{i} \right)  \, d\bar{v}
\end{align}
$$
We move along the particle trajectories
$$
\begin{align}
\frac{ \partial N_{s} }{ \partial t } &= \sum_{i=1}^{N_{0}} \left\{ \frac{ \partial N_{s} }{ \partial \bar{x}_{i} } \frac{ \partial \bar{x}_{i} }{ \partial t } + \frac{ \partial N_{s} }{ \partial \bar{v}_{i} } \frac{ \partial \bar{v}_{i} }{ \partial t } \right\} = \\[.8em]
 &= \sum_{i=1}^{N_{0}} \left\{ \frac{ \partial \bar{x}_{i} }{ \partial t } \frac{ \partial }{ \partial \bar{x}_{i} } \left[ \sum_{j=1}^{N_{0}} \delta \left( \bar{x} - \bar{x}_{j} \right) \, \delta\left( \bar{v} - \bar{v}_{j} \right)   \right]  +  \frac{ \partial \bar{v}_{i} }{ \partial t } \frac{ \partial  }{ \partial \bar{v}_{i} } \left[ \sum_{j=1}^{N_{0}} \delta \left( \bar{x} - \bar{x}_{j} \right) \, \delta\left( \bar{v} - \bar{v}_{j} \right)   \right] \right\}
\end{align}
$$
Here only the $i$ index survives, while all the mixed-indices terms are zero
$$
 \frac{ \partial N_{s} }{ \partial t } = \sum_{i=1}^{N_{0}} \left\{ \dot{\bar{x}}_{i} \frac{ \partial }{ \partial \bar{x}_{i} }  \delta \left( \bar{x} - \bar{x}_{i} \right) \, \delta\left( \bar{v} - \bar{v}_{i} \right)  +  \dot{\bar{v}}_{i} \frac{ \partial  }{ \partial \bar{v}_{i} }  \delta \left( \bar{x} - \bar{x}_{i} \right) \, \delta\left( \bar{v} - \bar{v}_{i} \right) \right\} 
$$
We use the equations of motion
$$
\begin{align}
\frac{ \partial N_{s} }{ \partial t } = &\sum_{i=0}^{N_{0}} \bar{v}_{i} \cdot \nabla_{\bar{x}_{i}} \delta (\bar{x}-\bar{x}_{i}) \, \delta(\bar{v}-\bar{v}_{i }) \,+  \\[.6em]
&+ \sum_{i=0}^{N_{0}} \frac{q_{s}}{m_{s}} \left[ \bar{E}_{m} (\bar{x}_{i},t) + \frac{\bar{v}\times \bar{B}_{m}(\bar{x}_{i},t)}{c}\right] \cdot \nabla_{\bar{v}_{i}} \delta (\bar{x}-\bar{x}_{i}) \, \delta(\bar{v}-\bar{v}_{i })
\end{align}
$$
We exchange the gradient derivative from the particles position to the general coordinate 
$$
\nabla_{\bar{x}_{i}} \, \delta(\bar{x}-\bar{x}_{i}) =  - \nabla_{\bar{x}} \, \delta(\bar{x}-\bar{x}_{i})
$$
and the same is valid for $\bar{v}$ and $\bar{v}_{i}$. 
So we have
$$
\begin{align}
\frac{ \partial N_{s} }{ \partial t } &= -\sum_{i=0}^{N_{0}} \bar{v}_{i} \cdot \nabla_{\bar{x}}\, \delta (\bar{x}-\bar{x}_{i}) \, \delta(\bar{v}-\bar{v}_{i }) \,+  \\[.6em]
&\ \ \ \ -\sum_{i=0}^{N_{0}} \frac{q_{s}}{m_{s}} \left[ \bar{E}_{m} (\bar{x}_{i},t) + \frac{\bar{v}\times \bar{B}_{m}(\bar{x}_{i},t)}{c}\right] \cdot \nabla_{\bar{v}} \, \delta (\bar{x}-\bar{x}_{i}) \, \delta(\bar{v}-\bar{v}_{i }) = \\[.8em]
&= -\sum_{i=0}^{N_{0}} \nabla_{\bar{x}} \cdot \left[\bar{v}_{i} \, \delta (\bar{x}-\bar{x}_{i}) \, \delta(\bar{v}-\bar{v}_{i })\right] \,+  \\[.6em]
&\ \ \ \ -\sum_{i=0}^{N_{0}} \nabla_{\bar{v}} \cdot \left\{ \frac{q_{s}}{m_{s}} \left[ \bar{E}_{m} (\bar{x}_{i},t) + \frac{\bar{v}\times \bar{B}_{m}(\bar{x}_{i},t)}{c}\right] \cdot  \delta (\bar{x}-\bar{x}_{i}) \, \delta(\bar{v}-\bar{v}_{i }) \right\} 
\end{align}
$$
Now we use property n.$4$ two times 
("$\bar{v}_{i} \times \bar{B} (\bar{x}_{i},t) = \bar{v}_{i}\,\delta(\bar{v} - \bar{v}_{i})\times \bar{B}(\bar{x}_{i},t)\,\delta (\bar{x} - \bar{x}_{i})$")
and we get
$$
\begin{align}
\frac{ \partial N_{s} }{ \partial t } &= -\sum_{i=0}^{N_{0}} \nabla_{\bar{x}} \cdot \left[\bar{v} \, \delta (\bar{x}-\bar{x}_{i}) \, \delta(\bar{v}-\bar{v}_{i })\right] \,+  \\[.6em]
&\ \ \ \  - \sum_{i=0}^{N_{0}} \nabla_{\bar{v}} \cdot \left\{ \frac{q_{s}}{m_{s}} \left[ \bar{E}_{m} (\bar{x},t) + \frac{\bar{v}\times \bar{B}_{m}(\bar{x},t)}{c}\right] \cdot  \delta (\bar{x}-\bar{x}_{i}) \, \delta(\bar{v}-\bar{v}_{i }) \right\} =\\[.6em]
&= - \bar{v} \cdot  \nabla_{\bar{x}} \left[\sum_{i=0}^{N_{0}} \delta (\bar{x}-\bar{x}_{i}) \, \delta(\bar{v}-\bar{v}_{i })\right] \,+  \\[.6em]
&\ \ \ \  - \frac{q_{s}}{m_{s}} \left[ \bar{E}_{m} (\bar{x},t) + \frac{\bar{v}\times \bar{B}_{m}(\bar{x},t)}{c}\right] \cdot  \nabla_{\bar{v}} \left[ \sum_{i=0}^{N_{0}} \delta (\bar{x}-\bar{x}_{i}) \,  \delta(\bar{v}-\bar{v}_{i }) \right]
\end{align}
$$
Using the definition of $N_{s}$ we get the **Klimontovich equation** 
$$
\frac{ \partial N_{s} }{ \partial t } + \bar{v} \cdot \nabla_{\bar{x}} \, N_{s} + \frac{q_{s}}{m_{s}} \left[ \bar{E}_{m} (\bar{x},t) + \frac{\bar{v}\times \bar{B}_{m}(\bar{x},t)}{c}\right] \cdot \nabla_{\bar{v}} \, N_{s} = 0
$$
This equation is 
- 7 dimensional 
- non-linear
- EXACT (and impossible to solve)
- with $\delta(x)$ functions

Note:
$$
\left. \frac{dN_{s}}{dt}  \right|_{\text{orbit}} = \frac{ \partial N_{s} }{ \partial t }  + \frac{ \partial N_{s} }{ \partial \bar{x} } \frac{ \partial \bar{x} }{ \partial t } + \frac{ \partial N_{s} }{ \partial \bar{v} } \frac{ \partial \bar{v} }{ \partial t } = 0
$$
with the partial derivatives being the terms of Klimontovich equation.
We now use the ensemble average $\left< \  \right>$ 
$$
f_{s} (\bar{x},\bar{v},t) \equiv \left< N_{s} (\bar{x},\bar{v},t) \right> 
$$
so we can write 
$$
N_{s} (\bar{x},\bar{v},t) = f_{s} (\bar{x},\bar{v},t) + \delta N_{s}, \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \text{ }\left< \delta N_{s} \right> =0
$$
same goes for $\bar{E}$ and $\bar{B}$ 
$$
\begin{align}
\bar{E}_{m} = \bar{E} + \delta \bar{E}, && \left< \delta \bar{E} \right> = 0 \\[.8em]
\bar{B}_{m} = \bar{B} + \delta \bar{B}, &&\left< \delta \bar{B} \right> = 0
\end{align}
$$
Now, substituting into Maxwell equations

We start from 
$$
\nabla \cdot \bar{E}_{m} = 4\pi \rho_{m}
$$
$$
\left<  \nabla \cdot \left( \bar{E} + \delta \bar{E} \right)  \right> = \nabla \cdot \bar{E}
$$
because derivatives ($\nabla \cdot$) do not depend on the ensemble ($\langle  \ \rangle$)
$$
\begin{align}
\left\langle  \rho_{m} (\bar{x},t)  \right\rangle & = \left\langle  \sum_{s} q_{s} \int N_{s} (\bar{x},\bar{v},t) \, d\bar{v}  \right\rangle  = \\[.8em]
 &=\left\langle   \sum_{s} q_{s} \int( f_{s}(\bar{x},\bar{v},t) + \delta N_{s}) \, d\bar{v} \right\rangle = \\[.8em]
 &=  \sum_{s} q_{s} \int \left(   \left< f_{s}(\bar{x},\bar{v},t) \right> + \cancel{ \left<  \delta N_{s}   \right> } \right)\, d\bar{v} = \\[.8em]
 &=  \sum_{s} q_{s} \int \left< f_{s}(\bar{x},\bar{v},t) \right> \, d\bar{v}  
\end{align}
$$
so 
$$
\nabla \cdot \bar{E} = 4\pi \sum_{s} q_{s} \int f_{s} \, d\bar{v}
$$
Little recap
1) we take the maxwell equations
2) we substitute the ensemble averaged quantities and the "perturbation"
3) we evaluate the ensemble average
4) we eliminate the ensemble averaged "perturbation"

for the other Maxwell equation we obtain
$$
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle \nabla \cdot \bar{B} &= 0 \\[.8em]
\displaystyle \nabla \times \bar{E} & \displaystyle= - \frac{1}{c} \frac{ \partial \bar{B} }{ \partial t } \\[.8em]
\displaystyle\nabla \times \bar{B} & \displaystyle= \frac{4\pi}{c} \bar{J} + \frac{ \partial \bar{E} }{ \partial t } = \\[.8em]
&=\displaystyle \frac{4\pi}{c} \sum_{s} q_{s} \int f_{s} v \, d\bar{v} + \frac{1}{c } \frac{ \partial \bar{E} }{ \partial t }
\end{cases}
$$
From the Klimontovich equation we have
$$
\frac{ \partial  }{ \partial t } \left( f_{s} + \delta N_{s} \right) + \bar{v} \cdot \nabla \left( f_{s} + \delta N_{s} \right) + \frac{q_{s}}{m_{s}} \left[ \left( \bar{E} + \delta \bar{E} \right) + \frac{\bar{v} \times \left( \bar{B} + \delta \bar{B} \right) }{c} \right] \cdot \nabla_{\bar{v}} \left( f_{s} + \delta N_{s} \right) = 0
$$
and we take the ensemble average 
$$
\begin{align}
0 &=  \frac{ \partial f_{s} }{ \partial t } + \nabla f_{s} + \bigg< \frac{q_{s}}{m_{s}} \bigg[ \bar{E} \cdot \nabla_{\bar{v}} f_{s} + \cancel{ \bar{E} \cdot \nabla_{\bar{v}} \delta N_{s} } + \cancel{ \delta \bar{E} \cdot \nabla_{\bar{v}} f_{s} } + \delta \bar{E} \cdot \nabla_{\bar{v}} \delta N_{s} +  \\[.8em]
&\ \ \ \ \ \,+ \frac{\bar{v}\times \bar{B}}{c} \nabla_{\bar{v}} f_{s} + \cancel{ \frac{\bar{v}\times \bar{B}}{c} \nabla_{\bar{v}} \delta N_{s} } + \cancel{ \frac{\bar{v}\times \delta\bar{B}}{c} \nabla_{\bar{v}} f_{s} } + \frac{\bar{v}\times \delta\bar{B}}{c} \nabla_{\bar{v}} \delta N_{s}   \bigg]  \bigg> =\\[.8em]
 &= \frac{ \partial f_{s} }{ \partial t } + \nabla f_{s} + \bigg< \frac{q_{s}}{m_{s}} \bigg[ \bar{E} \cdot \nabla_{\bar{v}} f_{s} + \delta \bar{E} \cdot \nabla_{\bar{v}} \delta N_{s} + \frac{\bar{v}\times \bar{B}}{c} \nabla_{\bar{v}} f_{s} + \frac{\bar{v}\times \delta\bar{B}}{c} \nabla_{\bar{v}} \delta N_{s}   \bigg]  \bigg> =  \\[.8em]
& = \frac{ \partial f_{s} }{ \partial t } + \nabla f_{s} +   \frac{q_{s}}{m_{s}} \bigg[ \bar{E} \cdot \nabla_{\bar{v}} f_{s} + \frac{\bar{v}\times \bar{B}}{c} \nabla_{\bar{v}} f_{s} + \bigg< \delta \bar{E} \cdot \nabla_{\bar{v}} \delta N_{s} \bigg>  + \bigg< \frac{\bar{v}\times \delta\bar{B}}{c} \nabla_{\bar{v}} \delta N_{s} \bigg>  \bigg] 
\end{align} 
$$
The following two terms 
$$
\delta \bar{E} \cdot \nabla_{\bar{v}} \delta N_{s} \ \ \ \ \ \ \text{and} \ \ \ \ \ \ \frac{\bar{v}\times \delta\bar{B}}{c} \nabla_{\bar{v}} \delta N_{s}
$$
are two non-linear terms.
To answer why they are not zero, let's assume that every perturbation is a sine wave, we will have
$$
\langle \sin\vartheta \rangle = 0\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \text{but }\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \langle \sin \vartheta \sin \phi \rangle \neq 0 
$$
The equation is usually rewritten as
$$
\frac{ \partial f_{s} }{ \partial t } + \nabla f_{s} + \frac{q_{s}}{m_{s}} \bigg[ \bar{E}  + \frac{\bar{v}\times \bar{B}}{c}  \bigg] \cdot \nabla_{\bar{v}} f_{s} = - \bigg< \frac{q_{s}}{m_{s}} \bigg[ \delta \bar{E} + \frac{\bar{v}\times \delta\bar{B}}{c}  \bigg] \cdot \nabla_{\bar{v}} \delta N_{s} \bigg>  
$$
or 
$$
\frac{ \partial f_{s} }{ \partial t } + \nabla f_{s} + \frac{q_{s}}{m_{s}} \bigg[ \bar{E}  + \frac{\bar{v}\times \bar{B}}{c}  \bigg] \cdot \nabla_{\bar{v}} f_{s}  = \left. \frac{ \partial f }{ \partial t }  \right|_{\text{collisions}} \approx 0
$$
the right term contains the granularity of the system and it is often neglected.
In plasma physics we have very little collisions, so we can safetly assume that this term is $\approx 0$.
We finally got the Vlasov Maxwell equations.

## Linearized Vlasov-Poisson system
So, we used 
- electrostatic approximation ($\bar{E} = - \nabla \phi$) 
- unmagnetized plasma ($\bar{B} = 0$) 
so the polished Vlasov Maxwell equations becomes the set of **Vlasov Poisson equations**
$$
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle\dfrac{ \partial f_{\alpha } }{ \partial t } + \bar{v} \cdot\nabla f_{\alpha } + \dfrac{q_{\alpha}}{m_{\alpha}} \bigg[  \underbrace{ \bar{E} }_{ -\nabla \phi }  + \cancel{ \frac{\bar{v}\times \bar{B}}{c} }  \bigg] \cdot \nabla_{\bar{v}} f_{\alpha} = 0 \\[.8em]
\displaystyle- \nabla^2 \phi  = 4\pi \sum_{\alpha} q_{\alpha} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f_{\alpha} \left( \bar{r},\bar{v},t \right) \,d^3v
\end{cases}
$$
At equilibrium we have Maxwell distribution 
$$
f_{\alpha 0} = \frac{n_{\alpha 0}m_{\alpha}^{3/2}}{\left( 2\pi k_{B}T_{\alpha 0} \right)^{3/2} } \exp \left[ - \frac{m_{\alpha}v^2}{2k_{B}T_{\alpha 0}} \right] 
$$
and 
$$
\begin{align}
&n_{\alpha 0} = \text{const}, && \bar{U}_{\alpha 0} = 0, && T_{\alpha 0} = \text{const} \\[.8em] 
&E_{0} = 0, && n_{i 0} = n_{e 0} = n_{0}, && \phi_{0} = K = 0
\end{align}
$$
without any loss of generality.

Now we perturb the system
$$
\begin{cases}
f_{\alpha} (\bar{r},\bar{v},t) = f_{\alpha 0} (\bar{v}) + f_{\alpha 1} (\bar{r},\bar{v},t) \\[.8em]
\phi(\bar{r},t) = \phi_{1}(\bar{r},t)
\end{cases}
$$
We substitute in the Vlasov equation and we have 
$$
\dfrac{ \partial  }{ \partial t } (\cancel{ f_{\alpha 0} } + f_{\alpha 1}) + \bar{v} \cdot\nabla (\cancel{ f_{\alpha 0} } + f_{\alpha 1}) - \dfrac{q_{\alpha}}{m_{\alpha}} \nabla \phi_{1} \cdot \nabla_{\bar{v}} (f_{\alpha 0} + f_{\alpha 1}) = 0
$$
If we neglected the nonlinear term
$$
- \dfrac{q_{\alpha}}{m_{\alpha}} \nabla \phi_{1} \cdot \nabla_{\bar{v}} f_{\alpha 1}
$$
we get the linearized form of the Vlasov equation.
$$
\dfrac{ \partial  }{ \partial t } f_{\alpha 1} + \bar{v} \cdot\nabla f_{\alpha 1} - \dfrac{q_{\alpha}}{m_{\alpha}} \nabla \phi_{1} \cdot \nabla_{\bar{v}} f_{\alpha 0} = 0
$$
Now we substitute in Poisson equation
$$
\begin{align}
\displaystyle- \nabla^2 \phi  &= 4\pi \sum_{\alpha} q_{\alpha} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f_{\alpha} \left( \bar{r},\bar{v},t \right) \,d^3v = \\[.8em]
&= 4\pi \sum_{\alpha} q_{\alpha} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} (f_{\alpha 0} + f_{\alpha 1}) \,d^3v = \\[.8em]
&= 4\pi \left[ e \underbrace{ \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f_{i 0} \, d^3v }_{ n_{i 0 } } - e \underbrace{ \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f_{e 0} \, d^3v }_{ n_{e 0} }  \right] + 4\pi  \sum_{\alpha} q_{\alpha} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f_{\alpha 1} \,d^3v = \\[.8em] 
&= 4\pi  \sum_{\alpha} q_{\alpha} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f_{\alpha 1} \,d^3v
\end{align}
$$
where we used the fact that $n_{i 0} = n_{ e 0}$ to eliminate the term with $f_{\alpha 0}$. 

So the linearized form for the Vlasov Poisson system is 
$$
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle\dfrac{ \partial  }{ \partial t } f_{\alpha 1} + \bar{v} \cdot\nabla f_{\alpha 1} - \dfrac{q_{\alpha}}{m_{\alpha}} \nabla \phi_{1} \cdot \nabla_{\bar{v}} f_{\alpha 0} = 0 \\[.8em]
\displaystyle - \nabla ^2 \phi  = 4\pi  \sum_{\alpha} q_{\alpha} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f_{\alpha 1} \,d^3v
\end{cases}
$$

### Vlasov solution (wrong)
Vlasov solved this system using the Fourier transform
$$
\begin{cases}
f_{\alpha 1} (\bar{r},\bar{v},t) = \hat{f}_{\alpha 1} (\bar{v}) \exp \left[  i \left( \bar{k}\cdot \bar{r} - \omega t\right) \right] \\[.8em]
\phi_{ 1} (\bar{r},t) = \hat{\phi}_{ 1}  \exp \left[  i \left( \bar{k}\cdot \bar{r} - \omega t\right) \right]
\end{cases}
$$
and
$$
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle-i \omega \hat{f}_{\alpha 1} (\bar{v}) + \bar{v} \cdot i\bar{k}\, \hat{f}_{\alpha 1} (\bar{v})- \dfrac{q_{\alpha}}{m_{\alpha}} i  \bar{k} \, \hat{\phi}_{1} \cdot \nabla_{\bar{v}} f_{\alpha 0} = 0 \\[.8em]
\displaystyle k^2 \hat{\phi} ^ 2 = 4\pi  \sum_{\alpha} q_{\alpha} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \hat{f}_{\alpha 1} (\bar{v}) \,d^3v
\end{cases}
$$
From Vlasov equation we have
$$
 \hat{f}_{\alpha 1} = \frac{- \dfrac{q_{\alpha}}{m_{a}}\, \hat{\phi}_{1} \, \bar{k} \cdot \nabla_{\bar{v} } f_{\alpha 0}}{\omega - \bar{k} \cdot \bar{v}}
$$
and using it in Poisson equation
$$
\begin{align}
 k^2 \hat{\phi}_{1} &= 4\pi \sum_{\alpha} q_{\alpha} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \left[ \frac{- \dfrac{q_{\alpha}}{m_{a}}\, \hat{\phi}_{1} \, \bar{k} \cdot \nabla_{\bar{v} } f_{\alpha 0}}{\omega - \bar{k} \cdot \bar{v}} \right] \, d^3v = \\[.8em]
&= - 4\pi \sum_{\alpha}\frac{k^2n_{\alpha 0}}{k^2n_{\alpha 0}} \cdot \frac{q_{\alpha}^2}{m_{\alpha}} \, \hat{\phi}_{1} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \left[ \frac{ \bar{k} \cdot \nabla_{\bar{v} } f_{\alpha 0}}{\omega - \bar{k} \cdot \bar{v}} \right]  \, d^3v  =\\[.8em]
&= - k^2 \hat{\phi}_{1} \sum_{\alpha} \frac{1}{k^2 n_{\alpha 0}} \cdot\frac{4\pi q_{\alpha}^2 n_{\alpha 0}}{m_\alpha}  \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \left[ \frac{ \bar{k} \cdot \nabla_{\bar{v} } f_{\alpha 0}}{\omega - \bar{k} \cdot \bar{v}} \right] \, d^3v =\\[.8em]
&= - k^2 \hat{\phi}_{1} \sum_{\alpha} \frac{\omega_{p \alpha}^2}{k^2 n_{\alpha 0}}  \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \left[ \frac{ \bar{k} \cdot \nabla_{\bar{v} } f_{\alpha 0}}{\omega - \bar{k} \cdot \bar{v}} \right] \, d^3v 
\end{align}
$$
and finally
$$
k^2 \hat{\phi}_{1} \left[ 1 + \sum_{\alpha} \frac{\omega_{p \alpha}^2}{k^2 n_{\alpha 0}}  \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \left[ \frac{ \bar{k} \cdot \nabla_{\bar{v} } f_{\alpha 0}}{\omega - \bar{k} \cdot \bar{v}} \right] \, d^3v  \right] = 0
$$
Now, the cases for  $k^2 = 0$  or  $\hat{\phi}_{1} = 0$  are not interesting
The integral in this expression is an integral with a divergence in  $\omega = \bar{k} \cdot \bar{v}$
Vlasov took the principal part of the integral missing all the important information which happen close to the pole of the integral.
