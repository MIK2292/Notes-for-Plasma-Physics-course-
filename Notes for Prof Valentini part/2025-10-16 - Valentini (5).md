# Laplace transform
$$
\mathscr{L} \left[ f(t) \right] (p) = \tilde{f}(p) = \int_{0}^{\infty} f(t) e^{-pt}\, dt 
$$
complex munber $p_{1}$ 
$$
\exists p_{1} \in \mathbb{C} \ \text{ s.t. }\ \int_{0}^{\infty} |f(t) e^{-pt}|\, dt < \infty
$$
is convergent.
If there exists two constants $M$ and $p_{0}$ $\in \mathbb{R}$ such that 
$$
| f(t)| \leq Me^{p_{0}t} \implies \tilde{f}(p)\, \text{ is well defined }\, \forall \,\mathrm{Re}(p) \geq p_{0}
$$
with $p_{0}$ "indice di crescita" or index of growth
The inverse transform is
$$
\mathscr{L} [\tilde{f}(p)]^{-1} (t) = f(t) = \frac{1}{2\pi i} \int_{p_{0}-i\infty}^{p_{0}+i\infty} e^{pt} \tilde{f} (p) \, dp
$$

## Vlasov equation in Laplace transform
We transform Vlasov equation in space using Fourier transform and in time using Laplace transform
$$
\int_{0}^{\infty} e^{-pt} \, dt \int _{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{e^{-i\bar{k} \cdot \bar{r} }}{(2\pi)^3} \, d^3 r \left[ \frac{ \partial f_{\alpha 1} }{ \partial t } + \bar{v} \cdot \nabla f_{\alpha 1} - \frac{\rho_{\alpha}}{m_{a}} \nabla \phi_{1} \cdot \nabla _{\bar{v}} f_{\alpha 0}  \right] = 0 
$$
$$
\int_{0}^{\infty} e^{-pt} \, dt \left[ \frac{ \partial  }{ \partial t }  \int _{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{e^{-i\bar{k} \cdot \bar{r} }}{(2\pi)^3} f_{\alpha 1} \, d^3 r + \bar{v} \int _{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{e^{-i\bar{k} \cdot \bar{r} }}{(2\pi)^3} \nabla f_{\alpha 1} \, d^3 r - \frac{\rho_{\alpha}}{m_{a}} \nabla _{\bar{v}} f_{\alpha 0}  \cdot \int _{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{e^{-i\bar{k} \cdot \bar{r} }}{(2\pi)^3} \nabla \phi_{1} \, d^3 r    \right] = 0 
$$
The **first integral** becomes
$$
I_{1} = \int _{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{e^{-i\bar{k} \cdot \bar{r} }}{(2\pi)^3} f_{\alpha 1} \, d^3 r = f_{\alpha \bar{k}} (\bar{v},t)
$$
The **second integral** becomes
$$
I_{2} = \int _{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{e^{-i\bar{k} \cdot \bar{r} }}{(2\pi)^3} \nabla f_{\alpha 1} \, d^3 r = \frac{1}{(2\pi)^3} \left( \int _{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{-i\bar{k} \cdot \bar{r} } \frac{ \partial f_{\alpha 1} }{ \partial x }   \, d^3 r , \ \int _{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{-i\bar{k} \cdot \bar{r} } \frac{ \partial f_{\alpha 1} }{ \partial y }   \, d^3 r, \ \int _{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{-i\bar{k} \cdot \bar{r} } \frac{ \partial f_{\alpha 1} }{ \partial z }   \, d^3 r\right) 
$$
and the integral in the $x$ direction is (solving by parts)
$$
\begin{align}
\int _{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{-i\bar{k} \cdot \bar{r} } \frac{ \partial f_{\alpha 1} }{ \partial x }   \, d^3 r& = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} dz\, e^{-ik_{z}z} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} dy\, e^{-ik_{y}y} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} dx\, e^{-ik_{x}x} \left( \frac{ \partial f_{\alpha 1} }{ \partial x }  \right) \\[.8em]
& = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} dz\, e^{-ik_{z}z} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} dy\, e^{-ik_{y}y} \left[ \cancel{ \left.f_{\alpha 1} e^{-ik_{x}x}\right|^{+\infty}_{-\infty} } - \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f_{\alpha 1} \left( -ik_{x} \right) e^{-ik_{x}x} \, dx \right]  \\[.8em]
& = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} dz\, e^{-ik_{z}z} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} dy\, e^{-ik_{y}y}  \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \left( ik_{x} \right) f_{\alpha 1}  e^{-ik_{x}x} \, dx   \\[.8em]
& = \left( ik_{x} \right) \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} dz\, e^{-ik_{z}z} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} dy\, e^{-ik_{y}y}  \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}  f_{\alpha 1}  e^{-ik_{x}x} \, dx   \\[.8em] 
& = \left( ik_{x} \right) \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}  f_{\alpha 1}  e^{-i\bar{k} \cdot \bar{r}} \, d^3r   \\[.8em]
\end{align}
$$
we can cancel that term because $f_{\alpha 1}$ goes to zero at infinity
for $y$ and $z$ we have
$$
\begin{align}
y:&&ik_{y}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f_{\alpha 1} e^{-i\bar{k} \cdot \bar{r}} \, d^3r \\[.6em]
z:&&ik_{z}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f_{\alpha 1} e^{-i\bar{k} \cdot \bar{r}} \, d^3r
\end{align}
$$
so 
$$
 I_{2} = i\bar{k}\, f_{\alpha \bar{k}}(\bar{v},t)
$$
The **third integral** becomes (using the same reasoning as before)
$$
I_{3} = \int _{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{e^{-i\bar{k} \cdot \bar{r} }}{(2\pi)^3} \nabla \phi_{1} \, d^3 r = i\bar{k} \,\phi_{\bar{k}}(t)
$$
So we have
$$
\int_{0}^{\infty} e^{-pt} \, dt \left[ \frac{ \partial  }{ \partial t }  f_{\alpha \bar{k}} (\bar{v},t) + i\bar{v}\cdot \bar{k} \,f_{\alpha \bar{k}} (\bar{v},t)  - \frac{\rho_{\alpha}}{m_{a}} \nabla _{\bar{v}} f_{\alpha 0}  \cdot i\bar{k} \,\phi_{\bar{k}} (t)    \right] = 0 
$$
Now we solve the time integral
The **first integral** becomes
$$
\begin{align}
\int_{0}^{\infty} e^{-pt}  \frac{ \partial  }{ \partial t }  f_{\alpha \bar{k}} (\bar{v},t)  \, dt &= \left. e^{-pt} f_{\alpha \bar{k}} (\bar{v},t) \right|^{\infty}_{0} - \int_{0}^{\infty} [-pe^{-pt} f_{\alpha \bar{k}} (\bar{v},t)]  \, dt \\[.8em]
&=  -f_{\alpha \bar{k}} (\bar{v},t=0) + p \tilde{f}_{\alpha \bar{k}} (\bar{v},p)
\end{align}
$$
The **second integral** becomes
$$
\begin{align}
\int_{0}^{\infty} e^{-pt} \left( i\bar{v}\cdot \bar{k} \right) \,f_{\alpha \bar{k}} (\bar{v},t) \, dt &= i\bar{v}\cdot \bar{k} \ \tilde{f}_{\alpha \bar{k}} (\bar{v},p)
\end{align}
$$
The **third integral** becomes
$$
\int_{0}^{\infty} e^{-pt} \left(  \frac{\rho_{\alpha}}{m_{a}} \nabla _{\bar{v}} f_{\alpha 0}  \cdot i\bar{k}  \right) \,\phi_{\bar{k}} (t) \, dt = \frac{\rho_{\alpha}}{m_{a}} \nabla _{\bar{v}} f_{\alpha 0}  \cdot i\bar{k} \,\tilde{\phi}_{\bar{k}}(p) 
$$
And the full equation becomes 
$$
-f_{\alpha \bar{k}} (\bar{v},t=0) + p \tilde{f}_{\alpha \bar{k}} (\bar{v},p) + i\bar{v}\cdot \bar{k} \ \tilde{f}_{\alpha \bar{k}} (\bar{v},p)\, - i \frac{\rho_{\alpha}}{m_{a}} \nabla _{\bar{v}} f_{\alpha 0} \cdot \bar{k} \,\tilde{\phi}_{\bar{k}}(p) = 0
$$

##  Poisson equation in Laplace transform
$$
- \nabla^2 \phi_{1}  = 4\pi \sum_{\alpha} \rho_{\alpha} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f_{\alpha 1} \left( \bar{r},\bar{v},t \right) \,d^3v
$$
We apply the transformations as before
$$
\int_{0}^{\infty} e^{-pt} \, dt  \int _{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{e^{-i\bar{k} \cdot \bar{r} }}{(2\pi)^3} \left[ \nabla^2 \phi_{1}  + 4\pi \sum_{\alpha} \rho_{\alpha} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f_{\alpha 1} \,d^3v \right]  \, d^3 r = 0 
$$
The **second term** becomes
$$
\begin{align}
\int_{0}^{\infty} e^{-pt} \, dt & \int _{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{e^{-i\bar{k} \cdot \bar{r} }}{(2\pi)^3} \left[  4\pi \sum_{\alpha} \rho_{\alpha} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f_{\alpha 1} \,d^3v \right]  \, d^3 r = \\[.8em]
&= 4\pi \sum_{\alpha} \rho_{\alpha} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \left[ \int_{0}^{\infty} e^{-pt} \, dt  \int _{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{e^{-i\bar{k} \cdot \bar{r} }}{(2\pi)^3} f_{\alpha 1} \,d^3r \right]  \, d^3 v =  \\[.8em]
&=  4\pi \sum_{\alpha} \rho_{\alpha} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \tilde{f}_{\alpha \bar{k}} \,d^3v
\end{align}
$$
Now we consider the Fourier transformation of the **first term**, which becomes
$$
\begin{align}
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{ -i\bar{k}\cdot \bar{r} } \,\nabla^2 \phi_{1} \, d^3r &= \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{ -i\bar{k}\cdot \bar{r} } \,\left( \frac{ \partial ^2\phi_{1} }{ \partial x^2} + \frac{ \partial ^2\phi_{1} }{ \partial y^2} + \frac{ \partial ^2\phi_{1} }{ \partial z^2}  \right) \, d^3r = \\[.8em]
&= \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} dz\, e^{-ik_{z}z} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} dy\, e^{-ik_{y}y} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} dx\, e^{-ik_{x}x} \left( \frac{ \partial^2 \phi_{1} }{ \partial x^2 }  \right) +  \\[.6em]
&+ \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} dz\, e^{-ik_{z}z} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} dx\, e^{-ik_{x}x} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} dy\, e^{-ik_{y}y} \left( \frac{ \partial^2 \phi_{1} }{ \partial y^2 }  \right) +  \\[.6em]
&+ \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} dx\, e^{-ik_{x}x} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} dy\, e^{-ik_{y}y} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} dz\, e^{-ik_{z}z} \left( \frac{ \partial^2 \phi_{1} }{ \partial z^2 }  \right)
\end{align}
 $$
Now, we solve the integral by parts two times and we use the fact that the electric potential (and also its derivative) goes to zero at infinity. We have  
$$
\begin{align}
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{ -ik_{x}x } \frac{ \partial^2 \phi_{1} }{ \partial x^2 } \,dx &= \cancel{ \left. \frac{ \partial \phi_{1} }{ \partial x } e^{ -ik_{x}x }  \right|^{+\infty}_{-\infty} } - \int_{-\infty}^{+\infty} (-ik_{x}) \frac{ \partial \phi_{1} }{ \partial x } e^{ -ik_{x}x } \, dx= \\[.8em]
&= ik_{x} \int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}  \frac{ \partial \phi_{1} }{ \partial x } e^{ -ik_{x}x } \, dx =\\[.8em]
&= \cancel{ \left. ik_{x} \phi_{1}  e^{ -ik_{x}x }  \right|^{+\infty}_{-\infty} } - ik_{x }\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty} (-ik_{x}) \phi_{1} e^{ -ik_{x}x } \, dx =\\[.8em]
&= - k_{x}^2 \int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}  \phi_{1} e^{ -ik_{x}x } \, dx
\end{align}
$$
And the same reasoning works for the other two integrals in $y$ and $z$
So we have
$$
\begin{align}
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{e^{ -i\bar{k}\cdot \bar{r} }}{(2\pi)^3} \,\nabla^2 \phi_{1} \, d^3r &=-k_{x}^2 \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{e^{ -i\bar{k}\cdot \bar{r} }}{(2\pi)^3} \, \phi_{1} \, d^3r -k_{y}^2 \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{e^{ -i\bar{k}\cdot \bar{r} }}{(2\pi)^3} \, \phi_{1} \, d^3r -k_{z}^2 \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{e^{ -i\bar{k}\cdot \bar{r} }}{(2\pi)^3} \, \phi_{1} \, d^3r = \\[.8em]
&=- \left( k_{x}^2 + k_{y}^2 + k_{z}^2 \right) \cdot\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{e^{ -i\bar{k}\cdot \bar{r} }}{(2\pi)^3} \, \phi_{1} \, d^3r = \\[.8em]
&=-k^2\, \phi_{\bar{k}} (t)
\end{align}
$$
Now we substitute these results and we get
$$
\int_{0}^{\infty} e^{ -pt } \, dt \left( -k^2 \, \phi_{\bar{k}}(t) \right) +  4\pi \sum_{\alpha} \rho_{\alpha} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \tilde{f}_{\alpha \bar{k}} \,d^3v = 0
$$
The first term actually is 
$$
-k^2 \, \tilde{\phi}_{\bar{k}}(p) +  4\pi \sum_{\alpha} \rho_{\alpha} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \tilde{f}_{\alpha \bar{k}} \,d^3v = 0
$$
and finally we have obtained the 
## Vlasov-Poisson equations in Laplace-Fourier space
$$
\begin{cases}
-f_{\alpha \bar{k}} (\bar{v},t=0) + p \, \tilde{f}_{\alpha \bar{k}} (\bar{v},p) + i\bar{v}\cdot \bar{k} \ \tilde{f}_{\alpha \bar{k}} (\bar{v},p)\, - i \dfrac{\rho_{\alpha}}{m_{a}} \bar{k} \cdot \nabla _{\bar{v}} f_{\alpha 0}  \ \tilde{\phi}_{\bar{k}}(p) = 0 \\[.8em]
\displaystyle-k^2 \, \tilde{\phi}_{\bar{k}}(p) +  4\pi \sum_{\alpha} \rho_{\alpha} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \tilde{f}_{\alpha \bar{k}} \,d^3v = 0
\end{cases}
$$
In particular, we can isolate the electric potential.
First we isolate the distribution function
$$
\begin{align}
&  \tilde{f}_{\alpha \bar{k}} (\bar{v},p) \left[ p + i \bar{v}\cdot \bar{k} \right]  = f_{\alpha \bar{k}} (\bar{v},t=0) + i \dfrac{\rho_{\alpha}}{m_{a}} \bar{k} \cdot \nabla _{\bar{v}} f_{\alpha 0}  \ \tilde{\phi}_{\bar{k}}(p) \\[1em]
\implies &  \tilde{f}_{\alpha \bar{k}} (\bar{v},p)  = \frac{f_{\alpha \bar{k}} (\bar{v},t=0) + i \dfrac{\rho_{\alpha}}{m_{a}} \bar{k} \cdot \nabla _{\bar{v}} f_{\alpha 0}  \ \tilde{\phi}_{\bar{k}}(p)}{ p + i \bar{v}\cdot \bar{k}} 
\end{align}
$$
Then we substitute it in the Poisson equation
$$
\begin{align}
&k^2 \, \tilde{\phi}_{\bar{k}}(p) = 4\pi \sum_{\alpha} \rho_{\alpha} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \left[ \frac{f_{\alpha \bar{k}} (\bar{v},t=0) + i \dfrac{\rho_{\alpha}}{m_{a}} \bar{k} \cdot \nabla _{\bar{v}} f_{\alpha 0}  \ \tilde{\phi}_{\bar{k}}(p)}{ p + i \bar{v}\cdot \bar{k}} \right] \,d^3v \\[1em]
\implies&k^2 \, \tilde{\phi}_{\bar{k}}(p) = 4\pi \sum_{\alpha} \rho_{\alpha} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{f_{\alpha \bar{k}} (\bar{v},t=0)}{ p + i \bar{v}\cdot \bar{k}}\, d^3v +  4\pi i \sum_{\alpha} \dfrac{\rho_{\alpha}^2}{m_{a}} \bar{k} \, \tilde{\phi}_{\bar{k}}(p) \cdot \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{ \nabla _{\bar{v}} f_{\alpha 0} }{ p + i \bar{v}\cdot \bar{k}}  \,d^3v \\[1em]
\implies&k^2 \, \tilde{\phi}_{\bar{k}}(p) \left[ 1 -  \frac{4\pi}{k^2} i \sum_{\alpha} \dfrac{\rho_{\alpha}^2}{m_{a}} \bar{k} \cdot \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{ \nabla _{\bar{v}} f_{\alpha 0} }{ p + i \bar{v}\cdot \bar{k}}  \,d^3v \right] = 4\pi \sum_{\alpha} \rho_{\alpha} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{f_{\alpha \bar{k}} (\bar{v},t=0)}{ p + i \bar{v}\cdot \bar{k}}\, d^3v  \\[1.5em]
\implies& k^2 \, \tilde{\phi}_{\bar{k}}(p)  = \frac{ \displaystyle4\pi \sum_{\alpha} \rho_{\alpha} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{f_{\alpha \bar{k}} (\bar{v},t=0)}{ p + i \bar{v}\cdot \bar{k}}\, d^3v }{ \displaystyle1 -  \frac{4\pi}{k^2} i \sum_{\alpha} \frac{\rho_{\alpha}^2}{m_{a}} \bar{k} \cdot \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{ \nabla _{\bar{v}} f_{\alpha 0} }{ p + i \bar{v}\cdot \bar{k}}  \,d^3v}
\end{align}
$$
