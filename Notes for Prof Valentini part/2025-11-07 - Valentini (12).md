# Energy paradox 
Vlasov system preserves both energy ad entropy.
Landau damping drains energy from the electric field. 
Where does the energy go?
How can we get it back reversing time since entropy is conserved?
It cannot go into thermal energy because we don't have collisions. 
What we did is the following
$$
\begin{align}
\tilde{f}_{\alpha k}  (\bar{v},p) &= \frac{f_{\alpha \bar{k}}(\bar{v},t=0)}{p+i(\bar{k} \cdot \bar{v})} + \frac{i\frac{q_{\alpha}}{m_{\alpha}} \tilde{\phi}_{k} (p) \bar{k} \cdot \nabla_{\bar{v}} f_{\alpha 0}}{p+i(\bar{k} \cdot \bar{v})} \\[.8em]
k^2 \tilde{\phi}_{k} (p) &= 4\pi \sum_{\alpha} q_{\alpha} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \tilde{f}_{\alpha \bar{k}} (\bar{v},p)  \, d^3v  
\end{align}
$$
and (using inverse Laplace Transform)
$$
f_{\alpha k} (\bar{v},t) = \int_{p_{0}-i \infty}^{p_{0}+i \infty} \tilde{f}_{\alpha k} (\bar{v},p) e^{ pt } \, \frac{dp}{2\pi i} 
$$
There is for this integral a group of poles which are also poles of $\tilde{\phi}_{k} (p)$ 
Using the same rotation as before
$$
f_{\alpha k} (\bar{v},t) = \sum_{j} f_{\alpha k} (\bar{v}) \exp(-i \omega_{j} t) + f_{\alpha \beta} (\bar{v}) \exp (-i \bar{k}\cdot \bar{v} t)
$$
Where in the sum we take care of the poles of $\tilde{\phi}$.
This first term is the landau damping contribution of $\tilde{\phi}_{k} (t)$.
However $f_{\alpha \bar{k}} (\bar{v},t=0)$ has an extra pole, the second term, which is the balistic contribution. This term oscillates and creates more and more wavelenghts in space as time advances.

The balistic term stores the energy lost by Landau damping. 
If we plug this $f_{\alpha k} (\bar{v},t)$ in the previous expression for $k^2 \tilde{\phi}_{k} (p)$, why don't we see the balistic pole in this solution?
(before we used a different method to reach a solution for this quantity)
$$
\begin{align}
k^2 \phi _{n} (t) &= 4\pi \sum_{\alpha} q_{\alpha} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}  f_{\alpha \bar{k}} (\bar{v},t) \, d^3v = \\[.8em]
&= 4\pi \sum_{\alpha} q_{\alpha} \left[ \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \sum_{j} f_{\alpha k} (\bar{v}) \exp(-i \omega_{j} t) \, d^3v + \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}  f_{\alpha \beta} (\bar{v}) \exp (-i \bar{k}\cdot \bar{v} t) \, d^3v \right]
\end{align}$$
We try to solve the second integral by applying a rotation such that $\bar{k} \parallel \hat{u}$
$$
\begin{align}
\mathcal{I } &\equiv \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f_{\alpha \beta} (u) \exp (-i ku t) \, du = \\[.8em]
  &= \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{n_{0}}{\sqrt{ 2\pi } v_{th\, e}} \exp \left( - \frac{u^2}{2 v_{th\,e}^2} \right) \exp (-ikut) \, du =  \\[.8em]
 &= \frac{n_{0}}{\sqrt{ 2\pi } v_{th\, e}} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \exp \left[ -\frac{1}{2} \left( \frac{u}{v_{th\,e} } +ik v_{th\,e} t \right)^2 \right] \exp \left( -\frac{k^2v_{th\,e}^2t^2}{2} \right) \, du
\end{align}
$$
Where in the last passage we used square completion method.
We substitute 
$$
y = \frac{u}{v_{th\,e} } +ik v_{th\,e} t
$$
and
$$
\begin{align}
\mathcal{I} &= \frac{n_{0}\cancel{ v_{th\,e} }}{\sqrt{ 2\pi }\cancel{ v_{th\,e} }} \exp \left( -\frac{k^2v_{th\,e}^2t^2}{2} \right) \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}  \exp \left( -\frac{y^2}{2} \right) \, dy  =\\[.8em]
&= \frac{n_{0}}{\cancel{ \sqrt{ 2\pi } }} \exp \left( -\frac{k^2v_{th\,e}^2t^2}{2} \right) \cdot \cancel{ \sqrt{ 2\pi } } =\\[.8em]
&= n_{0} \exp \left( -\frac{k^2v_{th\,e}^2t^2}{2} \right)
\end{align}
$$
We see **phase mixing**
$\mathcal{I} \sim \exp(-t^2)$ so we lose its contribution when we neglect the transient as we did before. 
HERE so the energy paradox is that the energy can only be recovered with a second order term which we neglected?

# Plasma wave echo theory
O'Neil, Ceuld, Malbourg, 1965
Here we show a toy model of their theory.
Perturbation of the electric field, one-dimensional system
$$
\begin{align}
 E_{1}(x,t) &= E_{1} \exp (- ik_{1}x - i\omega_{1}t) =  \\[.8em]
&= E_{1} \exp (- ik_{1}x - i\omega_{R}^{(1)}t + i\omega_{I}^{(1)}t) \\[.8em]
f_{1}(x,v,t) &= f_{1}(v) \exp (- ik_{1}x - i\omega_{R}^{(1)}t + i\omega_{I}^{(1)}t) + \\[.6em]
&\ \ \ \  + f_{1 \beta} (v) \exp (-ik_{1} x + ik_{1} v t)
\end{align}
$$
Where the second term is the balistic term and is not damped.
We say that the delay time between the first and the second perturbation in the electric field is
$$
\tau > \omega_{I}^{-1} 
$$
the second perturbation is 
$$
\begin{align}
 E_{2}(x,t) &= E_{2} \exp \left(- ik_{2}x - i\omega_{R}^{(2)}t + i\omega_{I}^{(2)}t\right) \\[.8em]
f_{2}(x,v,t) &= f_{2}(v) \exp \left[ - ik_{2}x - i\omega_{R}^{(2)}(t-\tau) + i\omega_{I}^{(2)}(t-\tau)\right] + \\[.6em]
& \ \ \ \ + f_{2 \beta} (v) \exp \left[-ik_{2} x + ik_{2} v (t-\tau)\right]
\end{align}
$$
The two balistic terms, which survive for a long time, couple togheter.
They can make constructive interference, which would be a second-order term.
$$
\mathcal{I} = f_{1\beta} (v) f_{2\beta} (v) \exp [i(k_{1}-k_{2}) x - i (k_{2}-k_{1}) vt + ik_{2}v\tau] 
$$
The contribution of this nonlinear term in the potential 
$$
\tau' = \frac{k_{2}}{k_{2}-k_{1}} \tau
$$
at $\tau'$ the exponent in the previous expression is not dependent on velocity and its contribution is visible.

## Energetics in wave-particles interaction
$$
\begin{align}
&\frac{ \partial  }{ \partial t } f (x,v,t) + v \frac{ \partial  }{ \partial x } f (x,v,t) - \frac{eE}{m_{e}} \frac{ \partial  }{ \partial v } f (x,v,t)  = 0 \\[.8em]
&\frac{ \partial E(x,t) }{ \partial x }  = 4\pi e \left[ n_{0} - \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}  f(x,v,t) \, dv  \right]
\end{align}
$$
with 
$$
\begin{align}
f(x,v,t) &= f_{0}(v) + f_{1}(x,v,t) \\[.8em]
f_{0} (v) &= \frac{n_{0}m_{e}^{1/2}}{\left( 2\pi k_{B}T_{e 0} \right)^{1/2} } \exp \left( -\frac{m_{e}}{2k_{B}T_{e 0}} v^2 \right) \\[.8em]
E(x,t) &= E_{1} (x,t) \\[.8em]
 n_{i 0} &= n_{ e 0} = n_{0}
\end{align}$$
And the linearized Vlasov equation is
$$
 \frac{ \partial  }{ \partial t } f_{1} (x,v,t) + v \frac{ \partial  }{ \partial x } f_{1} (x,v,t) - \frac{eE}{m_{e}} \frac{ \partial  }{ \partial v } f_{0} (v)  = 0
$$
We also want to linearize the Poisson equation, so we write 
$$
 E_{1} (x,t) = E_{0} \exp(\omega_{I} t) \sin(kx-\omega_{R}t) 
$$
using the approximation of $| \omega_{I} / \omega_{R}| \ll 1$ we get
$$
 E_{1} (x,t) \approx E_{0} \sin(kx-\omega_{R}t) 
$$
The poisson equation at time $t=0$ will be 
$$
\begin{align}
\frac{ \partial E_{1}(x,0) }{ \partial x } &= 4\pi e \left[ \cancel{ n_{0} } - \cancel{ \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}  f_{0} (v) \, dv } - \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f_{1}(x,v,0) \, dv   \right] =\\[.8em]
&= - 4\pi e\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f_{1}(x,v,0) \, dv 
\end{align}
$$
(the first two terms in the parenthesis are both $n_{0}$). 
Now we assume
$$
f_{1}(x,v,0) = f_{0}(v)\,g(x) 
$$
and
$$
\begin{align}
\frac{ \partial E_{1}(x,0) }{ \partial x } &= - 4\pi e\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f_{0}(v) \, g(x) \, dv =  \\[.8em]
 &= -4 \pi e g(x) n_{0}
\end{align}
$$
At $t=0$ we also have
$$
E_{1}(x,t)  = E_{0} \sin (kx)
$$
so using this into the previous expression yields 
$$
\begin{align}
kE_{0} \cos (kx) = -4\pi e g(x) n_{0}&& \implies&& g(x) = -\frac{kE_{0}}{4\pi e n_{0}} \cos(kx)
\end{align}
$$
we write it as
$$
\begin{align}
g(x) = \beta_{k} \cos (kx), && \text{with } \beta_{k} = -\frac{kE_{0}}{4\pi e n_{0}}
\end{align}
$$
So 
$$
 \begin{cases}
E_{1}(x,0) = E_{0} \sin(kx) \\[.8em]
f_{1} (x,v,0)= f_{0}(v) \beta_{k} \cos(kx)
\end{cases}
$$
We use the **method of characteristics** 
$$
 \frac{ \partial f }{ \partial t } + c \frac{ \partial f }{ \partial x } = 0
$$
with $f = f(x,t)$ and the following Initial Condition 
$$
 f(x,0) = f_{0}(x)
$$
We write
$$
\frac{d}{dt} f = \frac{ \partial f }{ \partial t } + \frac{ \partial x }{ \partial t } \frac{ \partial f }{ \partial x }
$$
which, for the case $\partial x  / \partial t  = c$ becomes
$$
\frac{ \partial f }{ \partial t } + c \frac{ \partial f }{ \partial x } = 0
$$
so
$$
\left. \frac{df}{dt} \right|_{\text{orbit}} = 0 
$$
$$
\begin{align}
 \int_{0}^t \frac{df}{dt'} \, dt' = 0&& \implies&& \frac{dx}{dt} =c && \implies&& x(t) = x_{0} + ct 
\end{align}
$$
and also 
$$
\begin{align}
 &\int_{0}^t \frac{d}{dt} (f(x(t'),t')) \,dt' = 0  \\[.8em]
\implies& f(x(t'),t') |^t_{0} =  0 \\[.8em]
\implies& f(x(t),t) - f(x(0),0) = 0 \\[.8em]
\implies& f(x(t),t) = f(x_{0},t_{0}) \stackrel{(IC)}= f_{0}(x_{0}) = f_{0}(x-vt)
\end{align}
$$
In our case
$$
 \frac{ \partial f_{1} }{ \partial t } + v \frac{ \partial f_{1} }{ \partial x } = \frac{eE}{m_{e}} \frac{ \partial f_{0} }{ \partial v } 
$$
with $f_{1} = f_{1}(x,v,t)$ 
$$
 \frac{df_{1}}{dt} = \frac{ \partial f_{1} }{ \partial t } + \frac{ \partial x }{ \partial t }  \frac{ \partial f_{1} }{ \partial x } + \frac{ \partial v }{ \partial t } \frac{ \partial f_{1} }{ \partial v }  
$$
with
$$
\begin{align}
 \frac{ \partial x }{ \partial t } = v, && \frac{ \partial v }{ \partial t } = - \frac{eE_{1}}{m_{e}} 
\end{align}
$$
and 
$$
0=\left. \frac{df}{dt} \right|_{\text{orbit}} = \frac{ \partial f_{1} }{ \partial t } + v  \frac{ \partial f_{1} }{ \partial x } -\frac{eE_{1}}{m_{e}} \frac{ \partial f_{1} }{ \partial v }  \approx  \frac{ \partial f_{1} }{ \partial t } + v  \frac{ \partial f_{1} }{ \partial x } - \frac{eE_{1}}{m_{e}} \frac{ \partial f_{0} }{ \partial t } 
$$
where we linearized and neglected the non linear term.
Finally we have 
$$
 \frac{df_{1}}{dt} = \frac{eE_{1}}{m_{e}} \frac{ \partial f_{0} }{ \partial v } 
$$
so 
$$
\begin{align}
 x(t) = x_{0} + vt, && v=\text{const}
\end{align}
$$
$$
\int _{0}^t \frac{df_{1}(x,v,t')}{dt'} \, dt' = \int_{0}^t \frac{eE_{1}}{m_{e}} \frac{ \partial f_{0} (v)}{ \partial v } \, dt'
$$
The first term is 
$$
\begin{align}
 \int _{0}^t \frac{df_{1}(x,v,t')}{dt'} \, dt' &= f_{1} (x,v,t) - f_{1}(x,v,0) = \\[.8em]
&= f_{1}(x,v,t) - f_{1}(x_{0},0) =  \\[.8em]
&= f_{1}(x,v,t) - f_{1}(x-vt,0) = \\[.8em]
&= f_{1}(x,v,t) - f_{0}\beta_{k} \cos [k(x-vt)]
\end{align}
$$
and substituting it we get 
$$
\begin{align}
f_{1}(x,v,t) &= f_{0}\beta_{k} \cos [k(x-vt)] + \int_{0}^t \frac{eE_{1}}{m_{e}} \frac{ \partial f_{0} (v)}{ \partial v } \, dt' =  \\[.8em]
&= f_{0}\beta_{k} \cos [k(x-vt)] + \frac{e}{m_{e}} \frac{ \partial f_{0} }{ \partial v } \int_{0}^t E_{1}(x_{0}+vt', t') \, dt'=  \\[.8em]
&= f_{0}\beta_{k} \cos [k(x-vt)] + \frac{e}{m_{e}} \frac{ \partial f_{0} }{ \partial v } \int_{0}^t E_{0} \sin (kx_{0} + kvt' - \omega_{R} t') \, dt'=  \\[.8em]
&= f_{0}\beta_{k} \cos [k(x-vt)] + \underbrace{ \frac{e}{m_{e}} \frac{ \partial f_{0} }{ \partial v }  \frac{E_{0}}{kv-\omega_{R}}  [-\cos (kx_{0} + kvt' - \omega_{R} t')]^t_{0} }_{ * }
\end{align}
$$
and
$$
\begin{align}
*&= \frac{e}{m_{e}} \frac{ \partial f_{0} }{ \partial v }  \frac{E_{0}}{kv-\omega_{R}}  [-\cos (kx_{0} + kvt' - \omega_{R} t')]^t_{0} = \\[.8em]
&= \frac{e}{m_{e}} \frac{ \partial f_{0} }{ \partial v }  \frac{E_{0}}{kv-\omega_{R}}  [\cos (kx_{0})-\cos (kx_{0} + kvt - \omega_{I} t)] = \\[.8em]
&= \frac{e}{m_{e}} \frac{ \partial f_{0} }{ \partial v }  \frac{E_{0}}{kv-\omega_{R}}  [\cos (kx_{0})-\cos (kx - \cancel{ kvt } + \cancel{ kvt } - \omega_{I} t)]   = \\[.8em]
&= \frac{e}{m_{e}} \frac{ \partial f_{0} }{ \partial v }  \frac{E_{0}}{kv-\omega_{R}}  [\cos [k(x-vt)]-\cos (kx - \omega_{I} t)]    = \\[.8em]
&= \frac{e}{m_{e}} \frac{ \partial f_{0} }{ \partial v }  \frac{E_{0}}{k}  \cdot \frac{\cos [k(x-vt)]-\cos (kx - \omega_{I} t)}{v-\omega_{R} / k}
\end{align}
$$
and finally
$$
f_{1}(x,v,t) = f_{0}\beta_{k} \cos [k(x-vt)] + \frac{e}{m_{e}} \frac{ \partial f_{0} }{ \partial v }  \frac{E_{0}}{k}  \cdot \frac{\cos [k(x-vt)]-\cos (kx - \omega_{I} t)}{v-\omega_{R} / k}
$$
(we will continue from here)

In linear vlasov we neglect nonlinear term 
$$
\frac{eE}{m_{e}} \frac{ \partial f_{1} }{ \partial v } 
$$
so lin. approx is broken when
$$
\left| \frac{ \partial f_{1} }{ \partial v } \right| \sim \left| \frac{ \partial f_{0} }{ \partial v }  \right| 
$$
which happens at time $\tau$
